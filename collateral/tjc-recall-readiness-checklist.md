# Medical Device Recall Readiness Checklist

**The Joint Commission Accreditation Survey Preparation**
Ambulatory Surgery Centers & Hospitals

---

## Purpose

This checklist is designed to help healthcare facilities — including Ambulatory Surgery Centers (ASCs) and hospitals — prepare for The Joint Commission (TJC) accreditation survey as it relates to medical device recall management. It reflects requirements under TJC Standard EC.02.01.01 EP 11 (Management of Recalls) and the transitional Physical Environment (PE) chapter under Accreditation 360: The New Standard.

**Instructions:** Review each item and place a checkmark (✓) in the final column if your facility is in compliance. Document gaps and assign an owner and target date for resolution. Retain this completed checklist as evidence of internal audit activity.

---

## Section 1: Governance & Ownership

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | A designated staff member is formally assigned responsibility for receiving, tracking, and acting on all medical device recall notices | Document in job description or policy; name should be on file | ☐ |
| 2 | A backup/alternate designee is identified in case of absence | Ensures continuity of recall management | ☐ |
| 3 | The recall management role is documented in the Medical Equipment Management Plan (MEMP) | TJC standard EC.02.01.01 EP 11 / new PE chapter | ☐ |
| 4 | Leadership has reviewed and approved the recall management policy within the past 12 months | Document approval date and approving authority | ☐ |
| 5 | Recall management responsibilities are included in annual staff competency assessments | Surveyor may ask staff directly about their recall responsibilities | ☐ |

---

## Section 2: FDA Monitoring & Notification

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | The facility actively monitors the FDA MedWatch Safety Alerts & Recalls database | www.fda.gov — Class I, II, and III device recalls | ☐ |
| 2 | Staff are subscribed to FDA MedWatch email alerts for medical device safety communications | Free subscription at www.fda.gov/safety/medwatch | ☐ |
| 3 | A process is in place to receive and review manufacturer recall letters and safety notices | Manufacturer notices often precede FDA posting | ☐ |
| 4 | Recall notices are reviewed within a defined timeframe upon receipt (e.g., 24–48 hours for Class I) | Define and document the timeframe in policy | ☐ |
| 5 | The facility tracks open/active recalls from the FDA recall database on a recurring schedule (e.g., weekly or monthly) | Document frequency in MEMP | ☐ |

---

## Section 3: Medical Equipment Inventory

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | A current, complete inventory of all medical equipment is maintained | TJC requires inventory regardless of ownership | ☐ |
| 2 | Inventory includes hospital-owned, leased, rented, physician-owned, and patient-owned equipment | All equipment used in patient care must be included | ☐ |
| 3 | Each inventory record includes: device name, manufacturer, model number, serial number, and location | Critical for matching against recall notices | ☐ |
| 4 | Inventory is reviewed and updated at a minimum annually (or when equipment is added/retired) | Document last review date | ☐ |
| 5 | Equipment managed by outside vendors or under service contracts is included or cross-referenced | Vendor-managed equipment is still subject to recall monitoring | ☐ |

---

## Section 4: Recall Response Process

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | A written policy and procedure for responding to medical device recalls is in place | Policy should reference FDA classification levels (Class I, II, III) | ☐ |
| 2 | The policy defines specific response timelines based on FDA recall class | Class I = immediate action; Class II = urgent; Class III = routine | ☐ |
| 3 | A process exists to immediately remove or quarantine Class I recalled devices from service | Devices must be pulled before further patient use | ☐ |
| 4 | A process exists to notify clinical staff and department heads when a recall affects their area | Communication chain should be documented | ☐ |
| 5 | Corrective action steps (repair, replacement, return) are documented for each recall event | Maintain a log of actions taken for each recall | ☐ |
| 6 | The facility notifies patients if a recalled device was used in their care, per CMS and legal requirements | Consult legal/risk management for patient notification criteria | ☐ |

---

## Section 5: Interdepartmental Coordination

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | Biomedical/Clinical Engineering coordinates with Materials Management on device recall response | These two departments must communicate promptly | ☐ |
| 2 | Radiology, Laboratory, Surgery, and other high-device-use departments are included in recall communications | Do not silo recall notices in one department | ☐ |
| 3 | A process exists for departments to report suspected device malfunctions that may trigger a recall | Encourage bottom-up reporting, not just top-down | ☐ |
| 4 | Risk Management and/or Compliance are notified of all Class I and Class II recalls | Essential for liability and regulatory tracking | ☐ |
| 5 | Purchasing/Supply Chain is notified to halt reordering of recalled devices | Prevent re-stocking of affected items | ☐ |

---

## Section 6: Documentation & Audit Trail

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | A recall log or tracking system is maintained with: recall date, device details, FDA class, action taken, and closure date | This is the primary evidence surveyors will review | ☐ |
| 2 | All recall notices (FDA and manufacturer) are retained on file for a minimum of 3 years (or per facility policy) | Retain even for recalls where no affected devices were found | ☐ |
| 3 | Documentation confirms each recall was cross-referenced against the facility equipment inventory | Must show evidence the inventory was checked | ☐ |
| 4 | Closed recalls are documented with confirmation that all affected devices were addressed | Surveyors look for closed-loop documentation | ☐ |
| 5 | Periodic internal audits are conducted to verify recall compliance | TJC recommends conducting internal audits on recall follow-through | ☐ |

---

## Section 7: Staff Training & Competency

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | Staff responsible for recall management have received training on the recall policy and procedures | Document training dates and attendees | ☐ |
| 2 | Department managers can describe the recall notification and response process when asked | Surveyors may conduct impromptu staff interviews | ☐ |
| 3 | New employee orientation includes information on how to report potential device safety concerns | All staff are considered part of the safety surveillance system | ☐ |
| 4 | Annual refresher training on recall management is provided and documented | Training records must be available for surveyor review | ☐ |

---

## Section 8: ASC-Specific Considerations

| # | Checklist Item | Notes / Reference | ✓ |
|---|----------------|-------------------|---|
| 1 | Surgical instruments, implants, and single-use devices are included in recall monitoring | High-risk category for ASCs — particularly Class I implant recalls | ☐ |
| 2 | Surgeon-owned or vendor-loaned equipment brought into the ASC is tracked and subject to recall checks | Loaner trays and vendor-supplied implants must be verified recall-free | ☐ |
| 3 | The facility has a process to verify that vendor/rep-supplied devices are not under active recall before use | Confirm at time of delivery or before surgical procedure | ☐ |
| 4 | Sterilization equipment (autoclaves, washer-disinfectors) is included in the recall monitoring inventory | Equipment recalls affect patient safety indirectly | ☐ |
| 5 | The ASC monitors for TJC Accreditation 360 rollout timeline to ambulatory settings for updated PE chapter requirements | TJC has confirmed ASC-specific Accreditation 360 timeline forthcoming | ☐ |

## Survey Readiness Score Summary

| Readiness Level | Score Range | Recommended Action |
|-----------------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Survey Ready** | 90–100% items checked | Maintain current practices; monitor for TJC Accreditation 360 ASC rollout |
| **Needs Improvement** | 70–89% items checked | Address gaps within 30 days; assign owners to open items |
| **High Risk** | Below 70% checked | Immediate corrective action required; escalate to leadership |
