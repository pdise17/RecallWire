Acronyms & Definitions

Master these terms before engaging with healthcare buyers. Using correct terminology builds credibility.

Healthcare Industry Acronyms

GPO
Group Purchasing Organization
Organizations that aggregate purchasing power for hospitals. 96% of US hospitals use a GPO. Top 3: Vizient, Premier, HealthTrust.
IDN
Integrated Delivery Network
Health system that owns/operates multiple hospitals and care sites. ~576 IDNs in the US. Examples: HCA, CommonSpirit, Ascension.
AMC
Academic Medical Center
Hospital affiliated with a medical school. Higher device complexity, research protocols. ~400 in US.
ASC
Ambulatory Surgery Center
Outpatient surgical facility. ~6,000 in US. Lower complexity, typically Standard tier.
CAH
Critical Access Hospital
Rural hospital with ≤25 beds, 35+ miles from another hospital. ~1,300 in US. Limited budgets.
FTE
Full-Time Equivalent
Measure of employee workload. 1 FTE = 2,080 hours/year. Used in ROI calculations.
EHR/EMR
Electronic Health/Medical Record
Digital patient records system. Major vendors: Epic, Cerner, Meditech. Integration point for device tracking.
MMIS
Materials Management Information System
Inventory and supply chain software. Key integration for recall matching.
UDI
Unique Device Identifier
FDA-mandated barcode on medical devices. Critical for recall matching and patient tracking.
GUDID
Global UDI Database
FDA's database of device identifiers. Public reference for device lookup.
TJC / JCAHO
The Joint Commission
Primary hospital accreditor. "JCAHO" is deprecated. Surveys every 3 years. Major compliance driver.
CMS
Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services
Federal agency overseeing Medicare/Medicaid. Sets conditions of participation. Fines for non-compliance.

Regulatory & Compliance Acronyms

FDA
Food and Drug Administration
Federal agency regulating medical devices. Issues recall classifications (Class I, II, III).
21 CFR 806
Code of Federal Regulations
FDA regulation requiring manufacturers to report corrections and removals. Key compliance reference.
MDR
Medical Device Reporting
FDA requirement to report device-related deaths, injuries, malfunctions.
MAUDE
Manufacturer and User Facility Device Experience
FDA database of adverse event reports. Public data source for device safety.
SaMD
Software as a Medical Device
Software intended for medical purposes. Subject to FDA regulation and recalls.
QMS
Quality Management System
Documented processes for quality control. Required for device manufacturers.

Financial & Business Acronyms

ARR
Annual Recurring Revenue
Normalized annual contract value. Key SaaS metric. RecallWire Standard = $4,999 ARR.
ACV
Annual Contract Value
Total annual value of a contract including one-time fees prorated.
TCV
Total Contract Value
Full value over contract term. 3-year $50K/yr deal = $150K TCV.
CAC
Customer Acquisition Cost
Total cost to acquire a customer. Target: <$800 for Standard, <$5,000 for Enterprise.
LTV
Lifetime Value
Total revenue from a customer over relationship. Target LTV:CAC ratio >3:1.
NRR
Net Revenue Retention
Revenue from existing customers YoY including expansion/contraction. Target: >110%.
ROI
Return on Investment
Savings/cost × 100. RecallWire typical: 400-1,700% ROI.
TCO
Total Cost of Ownership
Full cost including implementation, training, maintenance. Use when comparing to manual processes.
TAM
Total Addressable Market
Total revenue if 100% market share. RecallWire TAM: ~$47M (realistic) or $108M (list price).
SAM
Serviceable Addressable Market
Market segment you can realistically reach with current go-to-market.
SOM
Serviceable Obtainable Market
Realistic market share you can capture. Often 5-20% of SAM for early-stage.

Industry Terminology

Healthcare-specific terms you'll encounter in conversations with prospects.

Term Definition Sales Relevance
Class I Recall Highest severity. Reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death. Urgency driver. "75+ Class I recalls in 2024 - 15-year high."
Class II Recall May cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences. Volume driver. Most recalls are Class II.
Class III Recall Not likely to cause adverse health consequences. Low urgency but still requires documentation.
Quarantine Isolating recalled products from usable inventory. Key workflow step. "Where are quarantined items stored?"
Disposition Final action taken on recalled product (return, destroy, correct). Documentation gap. "Can you show disposition records?"
Field Safety Corrective Action Manufacturer's action to reduce health risk (recall, modification, alert). International term. Same as recall in practice.
Lot/Batch Number Identifier for products manufactured together. Matching criteria. "How do you track lot numbers?"
Serial Number Unique identifier for individual device unit. Required for capital equipment, implants.
Consignment Inventory Inventory owned by vendor until used. Complicates tracking. "Who manages consignment recalls?"
Implant Registry Database tracking which patients received which implants. Critical for patient notification workflows.
Sterile Processing Department that cleans/sterilizes surgical instruments. Stakeholder for instrument recalls. Also called "Central Sterile" or "SPD".
Conditions of Participation CMS requirements hospitals must meet for Medicare reimbursement. Compliance driver. "Loss of CoP = loss of Medicare."
Survey/Inspection Regulatory visit from Joint Commission, CMS, or state. Trigger event. "When's your next TJC survey?"
Immediate Jeopardy CMS finding of imminent danger to patient health/safety. Most severe finding. 23-day termination timeline.

Deal-Specific Terminology

Sales and contracting terms specific to healthcare enterprise deals.

$ Pricing Terms

  • List Price: Published price before discounts
  • Net Price: Final price after all discounts
  • Volume Discount: Reduction based on # of locations/users
  • Multi-Year Discount: 5-15% for 2-3 year commitments
  • GPO Pricing: Pre-negotiated rates through GPO contract
  • Sole Source: Contract without competitive bid

C Contract Terms

  • MSA: Master Service Agreement - umbrella contract
  • SOW: Statement of Work - specific project scope
  • BAA: Business Associate Agreement - HIPAA requirement
  • SLA: Service Level Agreement - uptime/support guarantees
  • DPA: Data Processing Agreement - data handling terms
  • Auto-Renewal: Contract renews unless cancelled

P Procurement Terms

  • RFP: Request for Proposal - formal bid process
  • RFI: Request for Information - early-stage inquiry
  • RFQ: Request for Quote - pricing request
  • Vendor Vetting: Security/compliance review
  • Vendor Credentialing: Registration process (Vendormate, etc.)
  • Capital vs. Operating: Budget classification affecting approval

I Implementation Terms

  • Pilot: Limited trial at 1-2 locations before full rollout
  • POC: Proof of Concept - technical validation
  • Go-Live: Production deployment date
  • Cutover: Transition from old system to new
  • Hypercare: Intensive support period post-go-live
  • Change Management: User adoption and training
Pro Tip: HIPAA Compliance Every healthcare deal requires a BAA (Business Associate Agreement) before any PHI (Protected Health Information) is shared. Have your BAA template ready and know your security posture. Common questions: SOC 2 certification, HIPAA compliance, data encryption, breach notification procedures.

Deal Lifecycle

The typical journey from first contact to closed deal in healthcare enterprise sales.

Stage 1: Prospecting & Qualification

Duration: 1-2 weeks

Identify potential buyers, qualify fit, and secure initial meeting.

  • Activities: Outbound emails, LinkedIn, recall-triggered outreach, inbound lead qualification
  • Goal: Schedule discovery call with qualified prospect
  • Qualification Criteria (BANT): Budget exists, Authority to decide, Need is real, Timeline is defined
  • Exit Criteria: Discovery call scheduled with right stakeholder

Stage 2: Discovery

Duration: 1-3 weeks

Understand their current state, pain points, and requirements.

  • Key Questions:
    • "How do you currently manage recall notifications?"
    • "Walk me through your last Class I recall response."
    • "When's your next Joint Commission survey?"
    • "Who else is involved in this decision?"
  • Goal: Map pain points, identify all stakeholders, understand decision process
  • Exit Criteria: Pain confirmed, stakeholders identified, demo scheduled

Stage 3: Demo & Solution Presentation

Duration: 1-2 weeks

Show the product, connect features to their specific pain points.

  • Demo Focus:
    • Recall alert → inventory matching workflow
    • Guided resolution with sign-offs
    • Audit report generation
    • AI Assistant in action
  • Goal: Generate excitement, address objections, identify next steps
  • Exit Criteria: Positive feedback, request for proposal/pricing

Stage 4: Proposal & Negotiation

Duration: 2-4 weeks

Present pricing, handle objections, negotiate terms.

  • Deliverables: Formal proposal, ROI analysis, reference customers
  • Common Negotiations:
    • Multi-year discount (5-15% for 2-3 years)
    • Implementation fee waiver
    • Payment terms (net 30/60/90)
    • Pilot before full commitment
  • Goal: Verbal agreement on terms
  • Exit Criteria: Terms agreed, move to contracting

Stage 5: Vendor Vetting & Security Review

Duration: 2-6 weeks (often parallel with contracting)

IT and security teams review your compliance posture.

  • Common Requirements:
    • Security questionnaire (SIG, CAIQ, custom)
    • SOC 2 Type II report
    • Penetration test results
    • HIPAA compliance documentation
    • Business continuity/disaster recovery plans
  • Goal: Pass security review without deal-blocking findings
  • Exit Criteria: Security approval granted

Stage 6: Contracting & Legal Review

Duration: 2-8 weeks

Legal teams review and negotiate contract language.

  • Key Documents:
    • Master Service Agreement (MSA)
    • Business Associate Agreement (BAA)
    • Data Processing Agreement (DPA)
    • Service Level Agreement (SLA)
  • Common Sticking Points: Liability caps, indemnification, data ownership, termination rights
  • Goal: Executed contract
  • Exit Criteria: Signatures obtained, deal closed

Stage 7: Closed Won → Implementation

Implementation: 4-8 weeks

Transition to customer success for onboarding and go-live.

  • Standard Implementation: Self-serve onboarding, help center, onboarding call
  • Enterprise Implementation: Dedicated team, training sessions, custom configuration
  • Key Milestones:
    • Kickoff call (week 1)
    • Data import & configuration (weeks 2-4)
    • User training (weeks 4-6)
    • Go-live (weeks 6-8)
    • Hypercare period (weeks 8-12)

Common Delays & Causes of Failure

Anticipate these obstacles and prepare mitigation strategies.

Deal Killers (Causes of Lost Deals)

1 No Champion / Weak Champion

What happens: Deal stalls, emails go unanswered, no internal momentum.

Prevention: Identify and develop champion early. Ask "Who else should be in this conversation?" Multi-thread into the account.

2 Budget Not Secured

What happens: "We love it but there's no budget" after months of work.

Prevention: Qualify budget early. Ask "Is there budget allocated for this?" and "What's your fiscal year timeline?"

3 Lost to Status Quo

What happens: "We'll just keep doing it manually" - inertia wins.

Prevention: Create urgency with compliance risk, audit timeline, or active recall. Calculate cost of inaction.

4 Decision Maker Turnover

What happens: Your champion leaves mid-deal, new person resets process.

Prevention: Multi-thread into account. Build relationships with 3+ stakeholders minimum.

5 Competitor Wins on Price

What happens: PAR Excellence or manual "good enough" wins on cost.

Prevention: Sell value, not features. Focus on compliance risk, ROI, audit readiness. "PAR = $2,400 + $42,500 risk"

6 Security/Legal Blockers

What happens: Can't pass security review, legal terms unacceptable.

Prevention: Have SOC 2, BAA ready. Know your non-negotiable contract terms. Engage IT/legal early.

Common Delays

Delay Type Typical Impact Warning Signs Mitigation
Budget Cycle 3-9 months "We need to wait for next fiscal year" Align proposal timing with their FY. Offer pilot to secure current-year budget.
Procurement Process 4-12 weeks "This needs to go through procurement" Ask early about procurement requirements. Get on vendor list proactively.
RFP Requirement 8-16 weeks "We need to do a formal RFP" Help champion write RFP requirements. Get specifications locked before competitors see RFP.
Security Review Queue 2-8 weeks "IT security has a backlog" Submit security questionnaire early. Provide SOC 2 report proactively.
Legal Review 2-12 weeks "Legal is reviewing the MSA" Use their paper when possible. Pre-negotiate with your legal on common redlines.
Committee Approval 2-6 weeks "Needs to go to the governance committee" Understand committee meeting schedule. Prepare champion with talking points.
Pilot Extension 4-12 weeks "We need more time to evaluate" Set clear pilot success criteria upfront. Regular check-ins during pilot.
Competing Priorities Indefinite "We're focused on X right now" Create urgency with compliance deadlines. Offer to start small.
The "60-Day Rule" If a deal hasn't progressed in 60 days, something is wrong. Either: (1) you don't have a real champion, (2) there's no budget, (3) it's not a real priority, or (4) you're being used for competitive leverage. Re-qualify aggressively or move on.

Objection Handling

Common objections and proven responses.

Objection Response
"It's too expensive" "I understand budget is a concern. Let me share what other [similar-sized] health systems have found: the average recall takes 40 hours of staff time manually. At $50/hour fully loaded, that's $2,000 per recall. Most facilities handle 20+ recalls per year - that's $40,000 in hidden labor costs. RecallWire at $X actually saves money. Can I walk you through the ROI calculation for your specific situation?"
"We're doing fine with spreadsheets" "That works until it doesn't. When Joint Commission asks 'show me the disposition of all recalled items in the last 3 years', can you produce that in 30 seconds? When a Class I recall hits at 5pm on Friday, does everyone know exactly what to do? Spreadsheets work for tracking - RecallWire provides tracking plus guided workflows, sign-offs, and audit-ready documentation."
"We need to see an RFP" "Absolutely, we're happy to participate in a formal process. A few questions to help us prepare the best response: What are the must-have requirements? Who else are you evaluating? What's driving the timeline? Would it be helpful if I shared how other organizations have structured their requirements?"
"PAR Excellence is cheaper" "PAR is about $2,400/year - roughly $200/month less than RecallWire. But there's a critical difference: PAR tracks that you received and acknowledged a recall. RecallWire tracks what you did about it - quarantine, return, destroy - with guided workflows and sign-offs. When Joint Commission asks 'what was the disposition?', PAR users have an acknowledgement. RecallWire users have a complete audit trail. That $200/month difference is your compliance insurance."
"We need to wait for next budget cycle" "When does your fiscal year start? [Understand timeline] What would it take to secure budget for this year? Some options: a smaller pilot program, or positioning this as risk mitigation rather than new spend. If you have a Joint Commission survey before then, this becomes urgent rather than optional. What's your survey timeline?"
"Our IT team has concerns" "That's completely reasonable - security is critical for healthcare data. What specific concerns do they have? [Listen] We're SOC 2 Type II certified, HIPAA compliant, and our BAA is ready to go. Would it help to set up a technical call with your IT team and our security lead? We can also provide our security documentation upfront to accelerate their review."
"We need more time to evaluate" "I want to make sure you have everything you need to make a confident decision. What specific questions are still unanswered? Is there additional information that would help - references, a deeper technical review, a proof of concept? I'm also happy to connect you with a customer in a similar situation. What would be most valuable?"
"We had a bad experience with [competitor]" "I'm sorry to hear that. Can you share what went wrong? [Listen carefully] That's helpful context. Here's how we approach [specific issue] differently... Would you like to speak with a reference customer who had a similar concern and can speak to their experience with us?"

Retention Strategies

Keeping customers happy and growing accounts over time.

The Retention Formula

Retention = Value Delivered + Relationship + Switching Costs
Focus on all three: prove ongoing value, build deep relationships, and become embedded in workflows.

Retention Playbook by Stage

Days 1-90: Onboarding & Activation

  • Goal: Get customer to first value quickly
  • Complete implementation on schedule
  • Train all users (aim for 80%+ adoption)
  • Process at least one real recall through the system
  • Weekly check-ins during hypercare period
  • Celebrate quick wins - share with stakeholders

Days 90-180: Value Realization

  • Goal: Prove ROI and build habit
  • Conduct first Quarterly Business Review (QBR)
  • Present metrics: recalls processed, time saved, compliance score
  • Gather feedback, address any friction
  • Identify expansion opportunities (more locations, add-ons)
  • Request case study / testimonial if strong results

Days 180-365: Expansion & Deepening

  • Goal: Expand footprint and become essential
  • Second QBR with year-in-review
  • Propose expansion: additional locations, categories, RMaaS
  • Introduce to new stakeholders (succession planning)
  • Integrate with additional systems if possible
  • Begin renewal conversation 90 days before expiry

Renewal & Beyond

  • Goal: Secure renewal with expansion
  • Start renewal discussion 90+ days before expiry
  • Present value delivered: recalls handled, time saved, audits passed
  • Offer multi-year discount for commitment
  • Bundle expansion with renewal for better terms
  • Get executive sponsor confirmation of continued partnership
